Community Engagement

Service to others is one of Groton School’s founding principles.

The school motto, Cui Servire est Regnare (for whom service is perfect freedom), has inspired students to serve their communities, the nation, and the world. At Groton, we believe service is part of responsible citizenship, a way to build community, and a part of every person’s life-long learning and purpose.

A few years ago, Groton Community Service became Groton Community Engagement (GCE). The name reflects Groton’s belief in working alongside others as equals, rather than “serving” as superiors.

Today, students participate in community engagement in a variety of ways during the school year. 

For more information, contact Director of Community Engagement Elizabeth Phan.

  •    Days of Service   
  •    Afternoon Activities   
  •    Grants   
  •    GCE Board   

   Days of Service   

On these all-school Days of Service (twice per year), students elect to work alongside our neighbors at nursing homes and animal shelters, in community gardens and playgrounds, at food banks, women’s shelters, and various other locations and organizations.

   Afternoon Activities   

Each term, students may elect to participate in GCE as an afternoon activity. During the Fall and Spring programs, we volunteer with the Leominster Elementary Schools to create a fun, hands-on experience for STEAM engagement. In the winter, we volunteer at the Senior Centers nearby and Seven Hills to work with youth with disabilities. During all terms we go to the International Institute of New England (IINE) in Lowell to provide English tutoring to refugee and immigrant youth and adolescents. Our program also offers opportunities to volunteer at the migrant shelter in Ayer and a local preschool.

   Grants   

Lawrence Global Scholars Fund   
Upper Schoolers may propose a civic engagement project centered around global issues that affect humanity to build cross-cultural skills and leadership. Through collaboration with local organizations, projects will assess the needs of their diverse community to listen, engage, and empower them in the process of social change. 


Dwight Internship Fund 
Upper Schoolers have an opportunity to propose an innovative project whereby they collaborate with social agencies to advance the causes of economic development, community building, education, health, job training, or youth development programs in the student’s home community. 
 

   GCE Board   

The GCE board provides a leadership opportunity for students who are passionate about community engagement work. This is an opportunity for those who may not necessarily have time in their schedule to dedicate to an afterschool program to still have a voice and leadership role in creating opportunities outside of our academic and afterschool commitments. The GCE board recruits volunteers, creates on-campus volunteer opportunities, and spreads awareness about our program!
