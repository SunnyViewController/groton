Student Life

Life at Groton School

Groton is both a school and a home. At home, you do all kinds of things besides studying. That’s true here on the Circle, too. Classes are important, but so is fun.

Most of the time, fun is unscheduled. You’ll hang out with friends on the Circle or during a meal, take a spontaneous walk to the river, or listen to music in a dorm room. But Groton also builds fun right into the routine.

A Typical Day at Groton

Play and Pause Buttons for Slideshow
8:00 a.m. Chapel

Four mornings a week, the entire school community gathers in St. John’s Chapel for quiet reflection, music, and readings from great spiritual traditions of the world. The focus of each weekday morning service is a chapel talk delivered by a Sixth Former, teacher, or another member of the Groton community.

Read More about 8:00 a.m. Chapel
8:30 a.m. Morning Classes

After Chapel, the class day begins. Groton's curriculum comprises courses in ten departments, all taught by a dedicated faculty in classes with an average size of eleven students.

Read More about 8:30 a.m. Morning Classes
10:15 a.m. Conference Period

Students get a brief break from classes during Conference Period, which is a great opportunity to catch up with teachers and advisors. Milk, cookies, fruit, and coffee are often available at the cafe in the Schoolhouse's Sackett Forum.

Read More about 10:15 a.m. Conference Period
Mid-Morning: Roll Call

On Wednesday and Saturday mornings, the Sixth Form prefects host Roll Call in the Forum, an opportunity for students and faculty to make announcements to the entire student body.

Read More about Mid-Morning: Roll Call
Noon: Lunch

Students head to the Dining Hall for lunch. Renovated in 2019–20, the Dining Hall features an expansive servery where students can fill their plates with the cuisine of the day or visit the salad bar.

Read More about Noon: Lunch
1:00 p.m. Afternoon Classes

Classes resume after lunch with two blocks that last until 3:15. Classes follow a six-day color block schedule, with half days on Wednesday and Saturday.

Read More about 1:00 p.m. Afternoon Classes
3:45 p.m. Afternoon Activities

Every Groton student commits to an afternoon activity each term. Many students, from novice athletes to experienced players, join a sports team. Others participate in a theater production or the dance program or volunteer at local public schools, senior centers, and other organizations through Groton Community Engagement.

Read More about 3:45 p.m. Afternoon Activities
7:00 p.m. Clubs and Organizations Meet

Students have the opportunity to join and lead more than forty student-run organizations, many of which hold meetings after dinner and before study hall.

Read More about 7:00 p.m. Clubs and Organizations Meet
8:00 p.m. Study Hall

A time dedicated to homework and preparation, study hall is structured and supervised for students in the Second, Third, and Fourth Forms.

Read More about 8:00 p.m. Study Hall
9:45 p.m. Check-In

A Groton ritual for more than 125 years, check-in is when each dorm pauses to gather, tell stories, and sometimes share a snack called a “dorm feed.” A faculty member, known as the dorm head, lives in each dorm alongside the students.

Read More about 9:45 p.m. Check-In

 

Parlor

Imagine having fun at the Headmaster’s House. It happens every Thursday night at Groton, in a longtime tradition that we call Parlor.

Parlor is a casual get-together with students, Headmaster Maqubela, and Mrs. Maqubela. It’s a time to hang with friends, play ping pong and board games, have a snack, and take a break in a warm and inviting home. From 7:30–8:15 p.m., either Second, Third, Fourth, or Fifth Form students drop by—the forms rotate each week. Sixth Formers attend every week, from 8:15–9:00 p.m. At Parlor, students relax, get to know the headmaster, and eat some amazing cookies.

 

Surprise Holiday

Surprise Holiday is one of Groton’s most beloved traditions, a welcome break in the routine every term. It’s fun to see how senior prefects share the news...they get pretty creative. Students have emerged from morning Chapel to find green streamers, green golf carts, green cupcakes, even a small horse wearing green. One constant is the green jacket, worn for decades by Groton headmasters to signal Surprise Holiday. After students whoop with delight, many take buses to Cambridge, Boston, or a nearby mall.

 

Spring Fling

At the end of May, it’s time for dorm spirit and friendly competition. Students wear T-shirts representing their dorms and play games such as dodge ball, tug-of-war, hockey on soap suds, and a range of silly contests. The winning dorm earns a special pizza party, and some bragging rights.

 

Circlefest

Bounce, joust, and bungee. Picnic on burgers and pizza. Climb on inflatable games that dot the Circle with color. CircleFest is Groton’s spring carnival. This newer tradition encourages students to take a break from homework, AP tests, and weekend routines and to adopt a few new goals: to relax, to enjoy, to play. Circlefest typically ends with a movie chosen by students, projected on a giant outdoor screen. The springtime event connects campus families, students, faculty, and staff through several hours of pure fun.

 

Open Mic Night

There are all kinds of performances at Groton—from music and dance recitals to concerts and Chapel postludes. But students started open mic nights, and they remain the most laid-back opportunity for our campus performers. Anyone can perform, and many students find open mics the perfect place to gain confidence before an audience. It’s no wonder: there couldn’t be a more supportive group. They’ll laugh with a novice comedian, applaud the inexperienced singer, and cheer loudly for the brave young rapper, the haiku artist, the yodeler. It doesn’t matter: open mic is open to anything. No wonder it’s become a favorite evening pastime.

 

Weekend Activities

Saturday nights should never be boring: Groton offers a ton of weekend activities. Some you might expect—a dance, a campus play, a game night, a movie on the Circle.

But how about…go-cart racing, a haunted corn maze, or a trampoline park? Nearly every weekend offers several fun excursions off campus. On campus, the Saturday night schedule has included stargazing with a science teacher, a holiday concert in the Forum, and chocolate-making (in 3D-printed molds) in our Fab Lab. There are also faculty-driven “date-night taxis,” as well as shuttles to nearby shops, a movie theater, and a supermarket.
